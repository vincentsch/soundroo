# Music with a point of view

A collection should have tunes people remember. Put a short original hook near the beginning, repeat it enough to establish an identity, then change the setting around it. Build toward a chorus or drop, thin the arrangement for contrast, and make the return feel earned. A pile of notes and a long quiet introduction do not make a song.

For an open brief, vary the musical purpose as well as the instruments:

| Direction | What makes it work |
| --- | --- |
| Rock and roll | Boogie bass, a guitar riff, firm backbeat, blues vocabulary, a short answering lead and cymbal/fill punctuation. |
| Garage rock / pop-punk | Tight power chords, busy eighth-note bass, forceful drums and a big contrasting hook. |
| Modern pop | A memorable lead standing in for a vocal hook, verse/prechorus/chorus contrast, syncopated bass and deliberate space. |
| Indie guitar pop | Clean strummed/plucked guitar, rhythmic bass and a bright hook; crunch and cymbals can lift the chorus. |
| Disco / funk | Syncopated muted guitar, moving bass, crisp backbeat and repeated danceable motifs. |
| Electroclash | Rougher guitar/synth textures, punchy drums, tension and a decisive drop. |
| Soul / Afro-pop-inspired groove | Syncopated guitar or keys, interlocking percussion, bass answers and a clear melodic refrain. |
| UK garage / breakbeat | Skipping kicks, offbeat percussion, shuffled timing and short vocal chops when appropriate. |
| Work grooves | Steady rhythm, fewer foreground notes and slow changes in texture. They can be lively without demanding attention. |
| Drum and bass | A fast breakbeat with a slower harmonic pulse, a firm bass motif and a clear change in density at the drop. |

These are broad starting points, not templates to rename. Write different motifs, rhythms, forms and hooks. A user who asks for rock should receive guitar-led music, not ambient synth music with a rock label. For more unusual art, change those conventions deliberately.

Native guitar and drum presets let the core explore these directions without downloads. Instrumental pop uses its lead as the hook; processed spoken clips are a creative texture rather than a substitute for natural sung vocals. A work collection should intentionally reduce melodic density, while a listening collection can take more risks and have stronger contrasts.

The `Turn It Up` example collection demonstrates this broader palette. The softer `The Open Window` collection remains available with `--collection open-window` when creating an example library. Existing libraries keep their tracks and listening state.
